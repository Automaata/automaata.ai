In California, security guards are registered by the Bureau of Security and Investigative Services (BSIS) and employed by licensed Private Patrol Operators. Registration comes with a training clock that keeps running after the card is issued. Missing a deadline is a licensing problem for the guard and a liability problem for the company that scheduled them.

Before the card

  • Be at least 18.
  • Complete the 8-hour pre-assignment course: 3 hours of Power to Arrest and 5 hours of Appropriate Use of Force. This must be done before BSIS issues the registration.
  • Pass a criminal-history background check through the California DOJ and the FBI, submitted by Live Scan fingerprinting.

After the card

  • 16 hours within 30 days of the registration date: the first half of the skills training.
  • 32 hours within 6 months in total, which includes the first 16. The courses cover subjects such as communication, public relations, observation and documentation, liability and legal aspects, and arrest, search and seizure.
  • 8 hours every year of continuing education after that, for as long as the guard holds the card.
  • The registration itself is renewed every two years.

Why it matters for scheduling

Each of these is a date. A guard registered on March 1 owes 16 hours by March 31 and 32 by September 1; a guard registered two years ago has a renewal date; every guard has an annual continuing-education date. Course providers issue certificates that the guard must give to the employer to keep on file, so the employer's records are the evidence that the clock was met.

A credential system therefore has to do four things: hold each deadline per guard, warn before it arrives, store the certificate when it is met, and refuse to schedule a guard whose card has lapsed. The last one is the one most spreadsheets cannot do.

How Automaata does it. Guard cards, permits and training hours are tracked with their dates. Reminders go to the guard and the scheduler ahead of each deadline (Level 0). A guard whose credential is expired or whose training deadline has passed cannot be assigned by the scheduler until the record is updated, and the block is visible in the approval inbox so a supervisor knows why a familiar name is missing from the roster.

Other states

Requirements differ by state. Arizona, for example, requires 8 hours of pre-assignment training from licensed companies, and some states have no training mandate at all. A company that operates across state lines needs the rule set per state, not a single California template. Confirm current requirements with BSIS or the relevant state regulator; this guide reflects California's published fact sheet and may lag changes.

Sources: BSIS, "Security Guard Registration Fact Sheet"; BSIS security guard training requirements (BPC §§ 7583.6, 7583.8); course-provider summaries of the 32-hour skills curriculum.
